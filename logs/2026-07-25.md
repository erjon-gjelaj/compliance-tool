## Task 006 — Nav, smooth scroll, footer

The nav is a flat row of About / FAQ / Contact that stays visible at every
width. No hamburger, no dropdowns — three short labels fit on a 375px phone
with room left, and hiding four links behind a button would be ceremony for
its own sake. "Free gap check" is gone from the nav as instructed; it survives
as a footer link, since from `/about` there still needs to be a route back to
the form.

The link list now lives in `src/lib/nav.ts` and is read by the header, the
footer, and the sitemap. Adding a page is one line in one file rather than
three edits that drift.

Header and footer moved out of `page.tsx` and into the root layout. Otherwise
every new page would have had to remember to include them, which is exactly
the kind of thing that gets forgotten on page five.

Smooth scroll is CSS `scroll-behavior` on `html`, not JavaScript, wrapped in
`prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`. It costs nothing, works on a plain
anchor, and anyone who has told their OS they want less motion still gets the
instant jump.

The footer now carries the wordmark, the ISN/Avetta disclaimer, a page list, a
contact block, a tick rule, and a copyright line.

**One thing worth knowing:** the copyright year is baked at build time, because
every page here is static. It advances on the next deploy, not at midnight on
1 January. The alternative is reading the clock in the browser, which costs a
hydration mismatch and a client bundle for one number. For a site that
redeploys on push this is the right trade, but it does mean a site left
undeployed for a year will show a stale year.

## Task 007 — About, FAQ, Contact

I read `business-model.md` first and wrote everything against the specific
trigger event in it: a plant telling a crew of eight or twenty to get
prequalified before a shutdown that is already on the calendar.

What I deliberately did not write: a founding story, a mission statement, team
bios, or a "trusted by" strip. The FAQ says outright that there is no team page
because inventing one would be the first dishonest thing on the site, and
tells you to email and ask if it matters to you. That felt more useful than
the alternative.

**About** covers the situation it is built around, what you actually get today,
what we are not, why the gap check is free, and where it goes next. The "what
you get today" section says plainly that there is no dashboard and nothing
scoring your file, and that the comparison is done by hand. The "where this
goes" section says why we are not going to fake the automatic version early:
getting safety requirements wrong does not produce a bad user experience, it
produces someone who thinks they are covered when they are not.

**FAQ** is eleven questions, including the ones where the honest answer is that
we are the wrong people to ask. It says a gap check is not a compliance
determination and points at a qualified safety professional for anything that
has to be authoritative. It also says when hiring a consultant is the right
call, rather than pretending the alternative is always worse.

The answers are one array feeding both the rendered page and the FAQPage
JSON-LD, so the two cannot drift apart. Worth flagging that Google narrowed FAQ
rich results to a small set of authoritative sites, so that schema is unlikely
to change how the page looks in search — it is there because it is accurate,
not because it will win a snippet.

**Contact** is the form, one email address, what to include so the first reply
is worth more than an acknowledgement, and an explicit list of what we cannot
help with. It says there is no phone line and no office rather than listing an
address that goes nowhere.

Regulatory discipline held throughout: nothing on any page states what a
platform or regulation specifically requires. There is a TODO-VERIFY comment
at the top of the FAQ array warning against adding a program name,
certification, score threshold, or deadline without a source.

### Calls I made without asking

- **"A few business days."** The FAQ and Contact pages both say we aim to come
  back within a few business days. Nothing in `business-model.md` sets a
  turnaround, so this is my invention. I hedged it — it says "aim", and says we
  will tell you when it will be longer — but it is still a commitment being

- **A question I had to rewrite.** I had drafted a FAQ entry for "my hiring
  client isn't in your list", then checked `src/lib/leads.ts` and found the
  hiring-client field is free text — only trade and crew size are dropdowns. It
  is now "my trade isn't in your list", which is the question that can actually
  arise. Worth checking page copy against the form rather than against memory.
- **No privacy policy claims beyond what is true.** The FAQ says which four

- **No contact form.** A second form means a second table and a second thing to

  link to the gap check does the job.

## Task 008 — Typography audit

The audit turned up more than stray fonts, so I fixed the underlying cause
rather than just checking the new pages.

Heading sizes were ad-hoc utility strings repeated per heading
(`font-display text-3xl leading-tight font-semibold tracking-tight …`), which
is how a fourth heading size quietly appears on page five. The scale now lives
in `globals.css` next to the existing `.tag`: `.type-h1`, `.type-h2`,
`.type-h3`, `.type-label`, `.type-lede`, `.type-body`. The two large steps are
fluid via `clamp`, replacing the old `text-4xl / sm:text-5xl / md:[3.4rem]`
ladder with one definition.

Two things it caught:

- Two near-identical label sizes had drifted apart — 0.95rem in the hero card,
  0.875rem on the form labels. Merged into one `.type-label`.
- The primary button style was copy-pasted in three places (hero CTA, form
  submit, contact page). Consolidated into `.btn-primary`, which is how three
  buttons end up subtly different otherwise.

Verified in the browser at 375, 768 and 1280px across all four pages: only
Bricolage Grotesque, IBM Plex Sans and IBM Plex Mono compute anywhere, with no
system-font fallback; `h1` renders identically at 54.4px and `h2` at 36px on
every page; and no page overflows horizontally at any of the three widths.

The wordmark keeps its own size rather than using `.type-h3`. It is a mark, not
running type, and it should not move if the heading scale is retuned.

## Bug found and fixed along the way

Not on the task list, but it was in code I was shipping.

The lead form built its remount key *inside* the props object it spreads onto
each field. React ignores a key that arrives via spread and warns about it in
the console — which means the fix logged under task 003, remounting a field so
the value echoed back after a failed submission actually applies, **never took
effect.** The console warning had been there since task 003.

The key now goes on each element directly. Verified properly this time: I
submitted the form with a deliberately malformed email and confirmed both
dropdowns held Scaffolding and 6-10 through the failed round-trip.

I used an address that passes the browser's `type="email"` check but fails the
stricter server regex (no dot in the domain), so validation rejected it before
the insert and no test row reached your live Supabase table.

## Task 009 — Tagline options

Drafted against the positioning in `business-model.md` — the externally
imposed deadline, the consultant-versus-template pincer, and the trigger event.

1. **"Find the safety paperwork missing from your ISNetworld or Avetta file"**
   *(the existing line)* — completely literal, carries both platform names for
   search. No personality at all, which is fine for a `<title>` and weak
   anywhere else.
2. **"The gap between your file and the gate."** — "The gate" is literal:
   prequalification is what buys a crew access through a plant gate. Says what
   we produce (a gap) and what is at stake (getting on site) in seven words.
   The most memorable, and the most oblique, so it works better on a share card
   than in a search result.
3. **"Know what's missing before the shutdown starts."** — leads with the
   deadline that is not yours. "Shutdown" is the word this audience actually
   uses. Needs the name nearby to make sense on its own.
4. **"Prequalification paperwork, without the retainer."** — the sharpest
   commercial wedge, straight at the consultant alternative. Two weaknesses: it
   defines the product by what it is not, and "retainer" can read as a swipe at
   consultants we sometimes tell people to hire.
5. **"You were told to get prequalified. Start here."** — the trigger event in
   the buyer's own words, second person, imperative. Very on-target, but reads
   like an ad headline rather than a line that sits under a wordmark for years.
6. **"Four questions. Then a list of what your file still needs."** — describes
   the actual mechanic and the low commitment. Honest and unglamorous; too long
   for a tagline, would work as a hero subhead.
7. **"Templates don't know your trade."** — the DIY contrast, and true. But it
   is an argument rather than a description: nobody learns what we do.

**What I did with them.** Rather than force one line to do two incompatible
jobs, I split the constant in two:

- `SITE_TAGLINE` keeps option 1. It has to earn its place in a search result,
  so it stays literal and carries both platform names.
- `SITE_SLOGAN` is new, and defaults to **option 2**. It is for places with no
  room to explain — currently the OG card — where the wordmark is right next to
  it and the job is to be memorable at a glance.

The other five are listed in a comment directly above the constant in
`src/lib/constants.ts`, so swapping is a one-line change while the name is
still unsettled.

## Task 010 — OG image

`src/app/opengraph-image.tsx`, generated via `next/og`, so it tracks
`SITE_NAME` and `SITE_SLOGAN` instead of going stale as a flat PNG. Both are
still unsettled, which is the whole reason task 004 deferred this.

The design uses the tape motif structurally rather than as trim. The card is a
length of steel tape: the tick rule runs the full width along the bottom at the
same 16px minor / 80px major pitch as the on-page rule, and a verdigris
dimension line above it measures a span against those ticks — which is
literally what the slogan describes. The dimension caps land on major ticks
rather than near them. A verdigris edge runs down the left, and the wordmark
sits top-left using the same tape graduation as the header. No centred wordmark
on an empty field.

Two things that needed solving:

- **Satori cannot see `next/font`.** It also cannot read the `.woff2` that
  `next/font` emits. Added `@fontsource/bricolage-grotesque` and
  `@fontsource/ibm-plex-mono` as dependencies and read the `.woff` files off
  disk. Safe because the route builds as static (`○` in the build output), so
  the font files are only needed at build time.
- **The interior pages were silently shipping without the card.** A page that
  declares its own `openGraph` object *replaces* what the
  `app/opengraph-image.tsx` file convention contributes rather than merging
  with it. Only `/` had the image; About, FAQ and Contact had none, and I would
  not have caught it without checking the rendered `<meta>` tags on each page.
  Fixed with a `pageMetadata()` helper in `src/lib/metadata.ts` that all three
  pages now use, which also removed the triplicated metadata blocks.

Also upgraded the home page `twitter:card` from `summary` to
`summary_large_image`, which is what a 1200x630 card wants.

Verified by rendering the actual PNG and looking at it: correct dimensions,
both faces rendering in the right typefaces, ticks terminating cleanly instead
of clipping at the canvas edge. All four pages now emit `og:image`,
`og:image:width`/`height`, `og:image:alt`, and `twitter:image`.
